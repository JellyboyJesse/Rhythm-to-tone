Rhythm Into Tone

Build a loop as a rhythm, then speed it up until it fuses into one tone. The harmonic bars and circles are calculated from the same loop you hear. Every control has an LFO, and the sequencer can change any of them step by step.

II. Speed

Below about 10 hits a second you hear separate hits. Above about 20 loops a second the whole loop fuses into one pitch at the loop rate, and the pattern becomes its timbre. Snap to notes rounds the speed to the nearest semitone once it is a pitch.

III. Rings

Each ring spreads its hits as evenly as possible over its steps (a Euclidean rhythm). A ring with equal steps and hits is a plain pulse. An LFO on Rotate turns the ring smoothly, between steps.

V. The loop as circles

16

Fig. 3. Circles chained end to end draw the loop.

Circle n spins n times per loop. Its size and starting angle come from the pattern's nth Fourier coefficient. Chained together, the circles draw the pattern: each spike is a hit. More circles give sharper hits.

VI. Click and fusion

Too many overlapping clicks: some were dropped. Shorten the click or slow down.

Click pitch sets a formant. It stays fixed in Hz while the loop pitch moves, like a vowel. Each click keeps the pitch and length it had when it started, so LFOs on the click change it hit by hit. Detune gives each ring a slightly different speed, so the tone splits into separate beating voices. Shared vibrato wobbles every ring together, which helps the ear hear one sound.
